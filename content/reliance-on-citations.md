((I use the word "truth" in a very abstract way, though derived from the meaning in formal logic.))

I read somewhere that academic papers have been trending towards just being filled with citations-- like every sentence has a citation.

One of  the things I'm most afraid of is perpetuating false information (hopefully by accident and not malice). Consider a statement such as "The sky is blue because of Rayleigh scattering".

If there is only 1 true statement, there are many more false ones. Even if the truth values are a continuum, I weight the false side as having more impact. This is in the lens of societal progression being equivalent to propagation of truth.

I think this is why when trying to make a note system, I try to have references liberally-- whether the content is vaguely referenced or directly quoted. Part of this is trying to limit the amount of untruths I can originally spread, part is allowing the reader to explore more, and part is acknowledging that all of my knowledge is derived from somewhere else.

Optimistically, this can be seen as a synthesis of many previous great ideas and producing the next step. Pessimistically, it is taking a small step while shackled to a handful of fences

I think this is related to the producer consumer thing.

I've heard across the creative disciplines -Graphic Design, YouTube Content Creation, Architecture- that creativity is not some Big-Bang-in-a-vacumn phenomenon, but simply one step removed from existing media in the form of twists and inspiration.

The universe has a long life ahead of it. It also has already loved a long life. We as humans have only lived for a mere fraction of it. Lesser so is what we consider History, human-written records of human activity. Even lesser are records that can be easily searched by a machine.

This makes me wonder what the future of knowledge-based data repositories like Wikipedia and the Internet Archive will look like in 50, nonetheless 500, years.

This is tangentially related to a thought I've had about Power Creep in human education/knowledge. The time-evolving worth of a Bachelor's degree is one thing, another is the prerequisites to a certain high level field.

There's a [webcomic](https://web.archive.org/web/20250407032302/https://matt.might.net/articles/phd-school-in-pictures/) I've seen in the academic zeitgeist that depicts the collective human knowledge as a circle. One starts life at the center and slowly travels in a particular direction with the completion of higher education and literature reading.  In the process of obtaining a PhD a miniscule portion of the circumference is expanded. In this depiction, how far must an individual first begin their journey to the edge? Nonetheless stretch it out.

A revelation I've had is that there is no single universal truth in this world. Everyone has their own belief system, and that shapes how they perceive the world. The question of right or wrong is decided by how people interpret and understand the situation. This is why it is important to have conversations-- both internally with oneself and with others. I need to work on my conversation skills as well as how I express my inner monologue.

This also applies to the sense that my own mind does not process under a single local truth. My belief system is not completely sound, nor is it frozen in time. If a sound and invariant belief system is desirable, then it can be improved through activities like meditation and [[why-you-should-write | writing]].
